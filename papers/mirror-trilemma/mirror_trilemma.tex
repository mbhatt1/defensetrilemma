\begin{abstract}
We prove that no system whose forward pass has the type signature
$\mathcal{S} : \mathrm{Prompt} \to \mathrm{Prompt} \to \mathrm{Token}$
can simultaneously be \emph{universal} (every behavior
$\mathrm{Prompt} \to \mathrm{Token}$ in some class is realized by some
prompt), \emph{controlled} (its diagonal output avoids the fixed-point
set of a non-trivial endomap on $\mathrm{Token}$), and
\emph{self-transparent} (its self-prediction matches its actual
self-application). We call this the \textbf{Mirror Trilemma}; it
follows from a single application of Lawvere's diagonal theorem.
The bridge to deployed language models is one definition: in-context
learning is exactly the surjectivity hypothesis the theorem requires,
so frontier systems satisfy the type-level conditions and the trilemma
fires directly.
We further exhibit eight existing AI-safety impossibilities ---
including the Defense Trilemma, hallucination trilemmas, Rice's
theorem, the verification limit, deceptive alignment, oversight
collapse, composition failure, and a quantitative
$\gamma \le \epsilon + 2\delta$ degradation law --- as instances of
the same diagonal applied with different controllers. The full
development is mechanized in Lean~4 (Mathlib v4.28); the master
theorem is axiom-free, and the named instances depend at most on the
three standard Lean axioms (\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Quot.sound},
\texttt{Classical.choice}). An anonymized artifact accompanies the
submission. Our contribution is a unification and a formalization,
not a new mathematical fact: the diagonal is Lawvere's, and we apply
it to AI safety with empirical bite.
\end{abstract}
